# Related Work Synthesis

Status: `partial_bounded_reconstruction`

The current literature map shows five nearest neighbours.

Campos and Pereira (2008) is the closest CGA reform study. It simulates how the
2006 reform of the public employees' pension system affects retirement timing,
initial pensions, and CGA pension-related expenditure. This project differs by
reconstructing financing flows and accounting identities rather than simulating
only reform effects on expenditure.

Pereira and Rodrigues (2001) and the 2006 revised projection work provide
long-run pension-expenditure projection context. They are useful for the
ageing/sustainability frame and for separating CGA from Social Security models.
They do not provide a transaction-level reconstruction of contribution,
transfer, expenditure, residual, and consolidated-perimeter definitions.

Garcia and da Silva (2023) estimate macro determinants of Portuguese pension
expenditure using time-series econometrics. That work is a close expenditure
paper, but its unit is aggregate pension expenditure as a share of GDP. This
project instead treats institutional financing categories and source-defined
perimeters as the object of reconstruction.

Abreu and David study CGA financial analysis and highlight accounting
information dispersion and consolidation problems in the CGA context. This is a
close institutional-accounting neighbour. The added contribution here is to make
the definitions, ledgers, validation checks, and unresolved evidence gates
machine-readable.

The financialisation and private-pension literature supplies context for funded
pension channels and crisis-era reforms. The ESA and national-accounts sources
from Banco de Portugal, the European Commission, and ECB/Eurostat are not
academic novelty evidence, but they are essential for avoiding category errors
between cash balances, financial transactions, implicit liabilities, and fiscal
deficit measures.
