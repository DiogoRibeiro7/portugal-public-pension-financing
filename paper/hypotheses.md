# Preregistered hypotheses and falsification conditions

## H1 — Employee contribution remittance

After documented timing and classification adjustments, employee deductions legally attributable to CGA should reconcile with employee contributions recorded as received.

**Null-compatible result:** no persistent material unexplained gap.

**Evidence against:** a persistent residual supported by independent source records showing withheld amounts exceeding remitted amounts.

## H2 — Employer legal compliance

Employer contributions recorded as received should reconcile with the employer contribution liability implied by the law applicable to each employer class and year.

**Failure condition:** apparent gaps disappear after correcting legal rates, contribution bases, employer perimeter, or timing.

## H3 — Historical CGA funding architecture

Modern dependence on State Budget transfers cannot be attributed solely to the 2006 closure of CGA if material State-financing dependence is visible before the closure after controlling for the legal contribution regime.

## H4 — Post-2006 contribution reallocation

The entry of new public employees into RGSS after 2006 mechanically increased RGSS contribution inflows while CGA's contributor base aged and contracted.

The magnitude must be reconstructed rather than assumed from published political claims.

## H5 — Robustness of the combined CGA + previdential balance

The sign and magnitude of the combined balance may depend on accounting perimeter, consolidation rules and historical adjustments.

A published claim that the balance is negative in every year is supported only if it survives the preregistered alternative definitions.

## H6 — Banking transfer: inception valuation adequacy

Under the exact assumptions imposed by Decree-Law 127/2011, transferred assets should equal the present value of transferred pension liabilities.

**This is a replication hypothesis, not an economic-neutrality claim.**

Reject or qualify if final transferred assets differ materially from independently valued liabilities after documented adjustments.

## H7 — Banking transfer: sensitivity of actuarial adequacy

The 2011 equality may be sensitive to the statutory 4% discount rate, mortality assumptions, indexation and asset-realisation assumptions.

Report the full sensitivity surface. Do not select one discount rate because it supports a preferred conclusion.

## H8 — Banking transfer: State financing compliance

The State-specific transfers prescribed by Decree-Law 127/2011 should reconcile with pension and administrative costs for the transferred banking pensions.

A Social Security burden is classified as **uncompensated** only when expenditure cannot be reconciled with specific State financing, transferred assets, attributable investment income or timing adjustments.

## H9 — Banking transfer: long-run net burden

Define annual net burden as:

```text
special-regime pension expenditure
+ attributable administration
- specific State financing
- attributable asset income/drawdown treatment
```

The hypothesis is deliberately two-sided: the cumulative value may be positive, approximately zero or negative depending on legal and accounting treatment.

## H10 — Banking transfer: fiscal one-off

Replicate the claim that the transfer improved the 2011 ESA-95 headline deficit by about 3.5% of GDP while having no direct deficit effect under ESA-2010.

Failure to reproduce either treatment with official national-accounts data must be documented.

## H11 — Banking transfer: distribution of risk and benefit

The transaction extinguished specified bank pension responsibilities but left specified updates/complements with the banks. Quantify, where data permit, the liability relief transferred to the public sector net of assets surrendered.

Do not equate "liability extinguished" with "bank subsidy" unless the net economic value is demonstrated.

## H12 — Manuscript neutrality

The final paper must remain valid if the evidence shows any of the following:

- no material historical CGA remittance gap;
- a material remittance gap;
- full legal employer compliance but economically low historical funding;
- robust combined pension deficits;
- non-robust combined deficits;
- a neutral 2011 bank-pension transfer;
- an adverse 2011 bank-pension transfer;
- insufficient evidence for a strong conclusion.
